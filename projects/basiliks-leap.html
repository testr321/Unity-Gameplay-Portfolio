Basiliks Leap

NokiaJam6 entry built in Godot 4.2 on the Nokia 3310 starter template — an 84×48 pixel screen, a three-colour palette, 15 FPS, and an LCD shader. A grid snake turned into a platformer: gravity fall, breakable platforms, teleport gates, and a star to reach.

My Contribution

Part of a 4-person jam. I owned gameplay on the Nokia 3310 template. The player is a Snake.gd multi-cell body — gravity, stun, breakable tiles, and teleports sit on that grid snake, including leftover eat/grow and self-collision.

  • Gravity-fall and stun (Snake.gd) — tick-based heading, 6px drop per tick off-platform, wrap on X, stun after a 2-cell fall with facing-aware animation; no 180 reverse or diagonal
  • Breakable tiles (MainGame.gd) — TileMap layer 1 erase after 1.2s and respawn after 5s, frame-counted, queued so the same tile isn’t processed twice
  • Teleport gates (Teleport.gd) and star win (Star.gd) with a fly-up intro that locks input; end screen (EndMenu.gd) shows time survived and move count
  • Kept template eat/grow (Food.gd) and self-collision lose, plus audio hooks in audio_manager.gd

Technical Breakdown

Problem

Classic snake is a flat grid. The jam needed platforms, breakable tiles, and falling inside an 84×48, 15 FPS, pixel-snapped screen where one frame is 66ms — a multi-cell body that desyncs from the 6px TileMap looks floaty.

Solution

Timers are frame-counted, not wall-clock: (Engine.max_fps × N) × seconds_per_frame. gravity_drop() adds exactly one cell size on Y to every cell per tick. Position propagates cell-by-cell, then hazard cells are compared; landing after two cells of fall starts a stun that blocks input.

Result

The platformer stays grid-snapped — no floating-point drift, no mid-frame glitches — and hazards still read on the tiny Nokia screen.

Reflection

What Went Well

  • Frame-counted timers kept retro timing stable at 15 FPS
  • Gravity fall + stun added platforming depth to the snake body
  • Breakable tiles and teleports made each level readable on a tiny screen

What I Would Redesign

  • Add more levels — the mechanics deserve a longer arc than one stage
  • Decouple the end menu and restart flow from scene reloads
  • Extract the frame-timer helpers into a shared utility instead of inline math
